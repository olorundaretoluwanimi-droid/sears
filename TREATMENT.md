# What Happened to Sears? The Catalog That Built Small-Town America

≈ 20–22 min (length follows the material) · Episode 4 of the series · English · narrator: AI33 Pro / ElevenLabs "Storyteller Oxley" ·
1920×1080, 30 fps · house rules kept (big real pictures, people and their own words, warm look, red only for dates and prices,
gentle score, 47 px captions, no blank frames, no style name or FACTS pointer on screen) · **new this time: a different visual
grammar, built from the catalog itself, with far more motion graphics.**

## The question

For half a century, a farm family in a town like Carlinville, Illinois, could buy a watch, a plow, a Christmas doll or the
house itself from one book printed in Chicago. Sears had the future of shopping in its hands — twice. What did it build, and
how did it let that go?

## Three structures considered

1. **Five acts in order** (Kannapolis, Newton). Clear, but it is the shape viewers have seen twice.
2. **One parcel's journey**: follow a single 1918 order from a Carlinville kitchen table to the Chicago plant and back by rail,
   then jump forward. Very visual, but it squeezes everything after 1931 into an epilogue, and the decline is the searched question.
3. **Seven things Sears sold** (the plan): a watch, the catalog, a house, a store, a tower, the last catalog, a closing-sale sign.
   Each chapter opens on an animated **order blank** (catalogue number, article, year, price) and ends on the question the next
   object answers. The open and the end speak to "you", in your own house.

**Chosen: 3**, with structure 2's parcel kept as a recurring motion device (the route map that comes back in chapters 2, 3 and 7).
It is the plan's shape, it gives the series its third distinct form (acts → dated days → objects), and objects suit motion graphics.

## Logline and arc

A railroad agent sells fourteen-dollar watches by mail, his company prints the book that brings the city store to every farm and
small town — even the house — and a century later the people who own it let the book go, a year before Amazon begins.
Setup: watch → catalog → house (delight). Turn: store → tower (pride, first unease). End: last catalog → closing sign (disbelief,
sadness) → your house today (bittersweet warmth: much of Sears is still around you).

## Benchmark (grammar only)

- **Paper-and-map explainers** (documentary explainers that move a camera over real documents and maps): learn the physical
  desk, the highlighted line on a real page, routes drawn across period maps. Don't take: their branding, graphics package, music.
- **Cut-paper title sequences of the 1960s**: learn shapes and type that move with a printed, slightly imperfect rhythm.
  Don't take: any specific sequence, character or typography.

## The new look: "the catalog comes alive"

Kannapolis, Newton and Flint were built from framed photographs on a dark warm ground, with hard cuts, red date cards and stat
overlays. Sears is built from **the catalog's own furniture**, so it looks and moves differently:

| | Earlier episodes | Sears |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | dark warm brown, framed photo panels | **an aged catalog page** (cream paper, ornamental double rules, rounded caption cartouches taken from the 1921 page) for chapters 1–4; the paper **darkens** with the decline (5–7) to the series' dark desk; warm light returns in the epilogue |
| Chapter opener | red date card | **animated order blank**: handwriting fills *Catalogue No. · Article · Year · Price*, the price is stamped in red, the page **curls over** to reveal the chapter |
| Camera | hard cuts between layouts, ≤ 1.06× push | **one moving camera per chapter** over a large laid-out spread (pans, pushes, a few degrees of roll), parallax layers, cuts only on music beats |
| Graphics | stats over photos | **motion graphics made from catalog material**: engravings cut out of real pages float in 2.5D; headlines drop in like set metal type; price tags swing in; a period rail map with parcels running to small towns; a house assembled from numbered lumber; a stack of pages that grows; a US map of stores lighting up and going dark |
| People | portrait + name card | portraits set **in the catalog's own cartouche frames**; ordinary people through the **customer letters Sears printed in 1897** ("What the people say"), typeset as letters with their town and date |
| Then/now | side-by-side pair | a **ruler-wipe** across the same view (1921 catalog photo → today) |

Colour: paper `#E4D5B1` (fibres, foxing), ink `#2A2018`, catalog red `#B8322A` for prices, dates and numbers only;
archive photos keep a sepia grade; post-1970 photos stay in muted colour (they are colour photos of a colour era); the dark desk
`#15110D` for chapters 5–7. Captions: 47 px Source Sans 3 semibold, cream on a warm dark band at 80 %.
Type: catalog display **IM Fell English** (OFL), text **Source Serif 4**, prices/numbers **Old Standard TT** (OFL), source tags in a small mono.

## Signature shot (the native move)

**The house arrives by train** (chapter 3): the camera rides along the 1918 rail map from Chicago to Carlinville; a boxcar opens,
numbered lumber (stamped like the real boards) flies out and assembles into the Carlin model's elevation drawing from the 1918
catalog, the floor plan draws itself beneath it — then the drawing **becomes** the 1921 catalog's real street photo of Carlinville
with a ruler-wipe. Placed at the emotional peak of the first half.

Second memorable moment: **the map of stores going dark** (chapter 7), one light at a time, to five.

## Shot grammar per chapter (summary)

- Open: macro push across real catalog engravings (wrench, washer) laid on the paper, parallax; the order blank fills; page curl.
- 1: 1888 newspaper ad floats up; rail map of Minnesota; portraits in cartouches; type-drop "SEARS, ROEBUCK & CO." (1893).
- 2: a stack of catalog pages grows (322 → 1,400); a US rail map with catalogs running out of Chicago to towns; RFD and Parcel
  Post photos large; the conveyor photo with animated order slips; customer letters typeset; the Christmas page with toys cut out.
- 3: the signature shot; mortgage ledger in red ink; Carlinville photos large, ruler-wipe to today; 1940 census map of the Addition.
- 4: storefront photos large; a map of stores lighting up (counts where sourced); Craftsman and Kenmore engravings → today's photos.
- 5: the paper darkens; the tower rises as a vertical bar against the 1906 plant tower; height counter; first-warning headlines
  set as plain type (names of rivals only, no logos).
- 6: the Big Book pages fan out and fall away; red-ink ledger; the "1993 → 1994" calendar flip (fair framing on screen).
- 7: dark desk; store dots go out; the closing-sale photos large, full colour, still; numbers counted down.
- Epilogue: warm light; then-and-now of Carlinville; your house again; the end card on a catalog back page.

## Sound

Gentle score (house rule), different palette from earlier episodes: **parlour piano with a light ragtime lilt** (Salamander Grand),
celesta/music box for the Christmas pages, clarinet, soft strings, upright-bass pizzicato; a horn for the people. Chapters 1–4 in
warm major; 5 gains a slow string pad; 6–7 thin to solo piano in minor; the epilogue brings the opening melody back, slower.
Foley: paper slides, page curls, a pen nib on the order blanks, a rubber stamp for prices, a cash-register bell (once, on the
first $14), a train for the house, conveyor rattle under the plant, a store-door bell, a single light switch click per store going
dark (very low). Music ducks ~8 dB under the voice. −14 LUFS.

## Voice

Oxley (AI33 Pro). **New this time: breaths and sudden stops removed before the final render** — every line is cleaned (breath
detection on low-energy, noisy segments between words; long internal pauses shortened to ≤ 0.35 s; soft 15 ms fades at every cut),
and lines are joined with even gaps, so the read flows without gasps or abrupt stops. Every line is checked by speech-to-text.

## Rules for this episode

- Every object card shows a real catalog page or photo of that thing; no catalog pages after 1930 in full on screen (later Wish
  Books are told over public-domain pages).
- No stock "a Sears" photos: every store, house or building is named and placed.
- No villain for the decline; owners' decisions and what followed.
- Material: public domain, CC0 and CC BY only; anything else is listed in PERMISSIONS.md for the user.
